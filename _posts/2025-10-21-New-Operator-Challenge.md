## Now That You Have Your License, What's Next?

Passing the RAE and hearing your callsign for the first time is a thrill. The very next step is to turn theory into confident, repeatable on-air skills. To help you explore every corner of the hobby, we have put together a challenge list that nudges you through voice, digital, portable, and technical activities. Treat it like a scavenger hunt for radio experiences—tick them off, log what you learn, and share your progress with the club so we can cheer you on.

### Challenge: Tune Up and Talk to Someone on a Local Repeater
Program the closest repeater into your handheld or mobile rig, confirm the TX/RX frequency offset and CTCSS code, and make a friendly call. This is the easiest way to meet the local community and get comfortable with net etiquette.

### Challenge: Send Out a WSPR Signal and See How Your Signal Propagates
Use WSPR to transmit a low-power beacon and watch the spotting map light up. Experiment with power levels and antennas to observe how far your signal travels under different band conditions.

### Challenge: Spot a Pico Balloon
Listen for a Pico Balloon with WSPR. Tracking the journey sharpens your weak-signal listening skills and teaches you about propagation in the upper atmosphere.

### Challenge: Hunt a Fox
Join a fox hunt. Direction-finding builds portable know-how—setting up antennas quickly, understanding signal reflections, and keeping cool under time pressure.

### Challenge: Activate a POTA Park (Any Band)
Pack your station, head outdoors, and activate a Parks on the Air reference. Logging 10 contacts from a park combines field-craft with public outreach and is a superb confidence booster.

### Challenge: Activate a SOTA Summit (Any Band)
Take your gear to higher ground for Summits on the Air. Some summits are drive-up, others demand a hike, and every activation tends to draw a pileup of chasers.

### Challenge: Make 10 FT8 Contacts
Fire up WSJT-X and complete ten FT8 QSOs. Focus on mastering timing, power management, and logging so that digital becomes a reliable operating mode in your toolkit.

### Challenge: Make 10 Contacts on Each HF Band
Dedicate time to each band, learning how propagation shifts through the day. Keep separate logs for noting the time, location, and signal strengths so you can spot patterns.

### Challenge: Make 10 Contacts on Each VHF/UHF Band (2 m, 70 cm)
Explore beyond HF by scheduling QSOs on 2 m and 70 cm. Try simplex, repeaters, and different polarisation to understand how these bands behave in urban and rural environments.

### Challenge: Make 10 DX Contacts
Aim for stations outside Africa. This is where careful listening, strategic timing, and antenna optimisation pay off. Chase grey-line openings and watch clusters to maximise your chances.

### Challenge: Tune Up and Talk to Someone on a Satellite Repeater
Work a low-earth-orbit satellite using a dual-band handheld or a portable setup. Predict the pass, track the satellite, and capture the QSO—all while staying within the short window of opportunity.

### Challenge: Receive SSTV From the ISS or a LEO Satellite
Monitor for scheduled ARISS SSTV events or passes of slow-scan television satellites, capture the downlink audio, and decode the image. Compare your copy with other operators to see how antenna placement and pass geometry affect the results.

### Challenge: Send an Email Through Winlink
Configure Winlink and pass a message over radio. Whether via HF or VHF packet, this task introduces you to amateur radio’s role in emergency communications.

### Challenge: Compete in a SARL Competition
Pick a South African Radio League contest and submit a log. Even a few contacts teach contest operating rhythms, logging discipline, and band strategy under deadline pressure.

## Make It Yours

Use a checklist, spreadsheet, or logging software to track progress and capture lessons learned. Bring your questions to the next club meeting. There is always someone ready to help with equipment choices, antenna builds, and propagation puzzles. Above all, keep experimenting, keep logging, and keep having fun. The airwaves are now yours to explore. 73!
