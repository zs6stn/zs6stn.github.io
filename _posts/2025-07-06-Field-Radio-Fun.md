## Saturday Field Radio – Rietfontein Nature Reserve

**Saturday, July 5, 2025 – ZA-0082 (Rietfontein Nature Reserve)**

What better way to spend a crisp winter afternoon than with radios, trees, and good company? At 13:00 CAT, a small group of enthusiastic amateurs gathered at the Rietfontein Nature Reserve to participate in the **SARL Newbie QSO Party** and activate **POTA ZA-0082**.

## SARL Newbie QSO Party Meets Parks on the Air

![Keith and Dieter at the portable HF station](/assets/images/2025-07-05-keith-dieter.jpeg)  
*Keith ZS6Hi and Dieter ZS6DO working HF under the trees*

Keith (**ZS6Hi**) and Dieter (**ZS6DO**) brought along a compact HF station setup—table, radio, logging system, and all the essentials for a successful outing. This was no mere park picnic; this was serious radio business disguised as fun.

Their main goal? To get on the air for the **SARL Newbie QSO Party** and rack up some grid squares, all while putting ZA-0082 on the POTA map.

## Linked Dipole Build and Deployment

![Linked dipole installed and in action](/assets/images/2025-07-05-rogan.jpeg)  
*A field-deployed linked dipole stretching proudly into the blue winter sky*

Gerrit (**ZS6GVH**) and Rogan (**ZS6ION**) took the opportunity to build and deploy a **linked dipole** covering the 40m, 20m, and 10m bands. Using a lightweight fiberglass mast and guy lines, the antenna stood tall and steady in the field.

This type of antenna, with physical breakpoints ("links") for band changes, proved to be an effective and versatile choice for a portable multi-band operation.

![Adjustments being made to the dipole feed point](/assets/images/2025-07-05-dipole.jpeg)  


## Great Propagation, Clean Logs

The setup worked flawlessly. Conditions on 20m and 40m were good, with decent signals across the region. A number of QSOs were logged, including with other stations participating in the Newbie QSO Party. 

The exchange format—RS report, grid square, and license age code (e.g., “Golf” for three years)—made for quick and clear contacts. Being in a POTA park added extra motivation for hunters scanning the bands.
